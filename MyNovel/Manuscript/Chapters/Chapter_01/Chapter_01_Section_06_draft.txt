Maya ran. Her legs pumped over the uneven asphalt of the quiet street, each stride a desperate effort against the roaring silence where Tiffany’s laughter usually was. The memory of the roadside—the sudden stillness, the way her friend had been pulled from whatever spot she’d been standing—was a blinding, fragmented thing. She didn't look back; looking back felt like stepping into the vacuum that swallowed the sound of their conversation moments before.

Her heart hammered against her ribs, a frantic, trapped bird beating against bone. The familiar scent of vanilla, which always seemed to cling faintly to her own skin, was suddenly overwhelmed by a sharp, metallic tang of pure terror.

She reached the corner where Tony and Tiffany lived, stumbling slightly as she approached the front porch. The house was too still, too normal for the chaos erupting in her chest. Breath hitched in her throat, sharp and painful. She fumbled with the key in the lock, her fingers shaking so badly it took a clumsy attempt, then another, finally scraping open the door with a small, choked gasp of relief that tasted nothing like oxygen.

The air inside was cooler than the late afternoon heat outside, carrying the faint, comforting blend of cedar and soap that always clung to this home. Maya ignored the entryway, driven only by the urgent need for solid ground that wasn’t pavement. She moved past the living area, knowing exactly where she needed to go.

Tony’s room door was ajar, a sliver of deeper shadow beckoning her forward. She pushed through it, heedless of anything other than the looming presence of safety.

The sight that met her—Tony asleep in his bed, wearing only boxers—did absolutely nothing to register on her panicked consciousness. He looked deeply settled, an image of utter peace juxtaposed against the storm raging inside her. She didn't slow down.

A sudden, desperate surge propelled her forward. With a raw cry ripped from deep within her core, she launched herself across the room, tackling the nearest anchor: Tony.

She landed hard against him, collapsing into his side, not caring about the sheets or the bare skin beneath the fabric of his sleepwear. She coiled around him immediately, her arms gripping his torso with desperate force, burying her face somewhere near his shoulder, inhaling deeply—trying desperately to pull enough reality from him to keep herself afloat.

“Tony…” Her voice was barely a whisper, thin and shredded by shock. It sounded alien coming out of her own mouth.

Tony jolted awake instantly. His body tensed under the abrupt impact, his hand shooting out reflexively before he registered who—or rather, *what*—was clinging to him. The exhaustion and depth of his sleep seemed to vanish in an instant, replaced by a razor-sharp alertness mixed with profound concern as he realized Maya was draped over him like something fragile and broken.

He didn't flinch away; instead, his practiced reflexes kicked in, years of caring for people under duress guiding him. He wrapped one arm instinctively around her back, pulling her slightly off balance so he could support her weight without rocking her too much.

"Maya? Hey," Tony murmured, his hazel eyes wide and immediately assessing her condition, completely bypassing any attempt at understanding the immediate surrounding scene. "Sweetheart? What happened?"

He shifted carefully, angling his body to absorb her full weight against the mattress while keeping his movements slow and deliberate. He began rubbing gentle circles on her back, a soothing rhythm that spoke of patient care.

Maya only clung tighter. She whimpered, the sound ragged, and dug her fingernails lightly into his skin through the thin fabric of his boxers. Her breathing was shallow, hitching in painful gasps that caught in her throat.

“Tony… I…” she started, but the effort felt monumental, like trying to lift a boulder. She shook her head violently against his chest, refusing to let go. She couldn’t articulate *anything*. The image of the roadside, the van, the suddenness—it was all smeared together with terror.

Tony kept talking, his voice a low, steady rumble pitched perfectly to counteract the frantic drumming of her heart. “It’s okay, Maya. You’re here. You’re safe. Breathe for me, sweetie. Just breathe.” He didn't ask what was wrong; he just provided the necessary ballast until she could manage an exhale.

A tremor ran through her entire body, starting deep in her core and radiating outward, making it hard to keep her limbs still enough to hold onto him correctly. She pulled back just enough to look at him, her brown eyes vast pools of unshed tears, glassy with trauma. She tried again, forcing words past the lump of panic lodged in her throat. "I... I don't know..."

"Shh," Tony soothed, tightening his arm around her protectively, pressing his cheek lightly against her damp hair. "You don't have to tell me anything right now. Just let your body settle. Let me help you."

She squeezed her eyes shut, tears finally breaching the dam and spilling hot tracks down her cheeks. They mixed with the faint vanilla scent that seemed to emanate from her distress. She fought to speak again, to prove she wasn't breaking down completely, but every attempt dissolved into a choked, wet sound. She dug her fingers deeper into his side, rocking slightly as if trying to physically meld herself into the solid warmth of him.

“It… it was…” Her voice cracked on the syllable, catching on nothing. She inhaled sharply, a desperate little gasp that sounded like a sob being forcibly swallowed. She rocked against him again, needing the physical confirmation that he was real, tangible, unmoving.

Tony continued rubbing her back, never releasing the steady beat of reassurance. “Look at me, Maya. Look at me. You’re okay. We’ll figure this out together, alright? Just focus on my voice.”

The pressure built up behind Maya’s eyes until it felt less like sadness and more like tectonic plate shifting—a sudden, overwhelming breakthrough of pure, undiluted panic demanding release through speech, even if the words were wrong. She pressed her face back against his chest, taking another shuddering breath, and found the singular point of necessary articulation, the core fact that had knocked her world sideways.

“Tiffany is gone”
