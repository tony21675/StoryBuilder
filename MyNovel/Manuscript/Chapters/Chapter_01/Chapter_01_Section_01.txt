The late afternoon sun slanted across the asphalt of the front road, dappling the pavement in patches where the shade from the sprawling oak trees broke through. The sound of their footsteps was steady—a comfortable rhythm against the slight breeze carrying the scent of freshly cut grass from the neighboring lawns.

"Seriously, though," Tiffany said, kicking lightly at a loose pebble near the curb, her dark red curls bouncing slightly with the motion. "I think Mr. Harrison actually *enjoyed* grading those papers today. It was almost unsettling."

Maya snorted, adjusting the strap of her worn backpack as they passed a line of parked cars. "Unsettling? Tiff, he made us write an entire five-paragraph essay comparing themes in two poems about ducks. He practically glowed when he handed them back."

"He didn't just hand them back," Tiffany corrected, turning her head to look at Maya with a playful grin that always seemed to summon dimples. "He beamed. Like he’d finally achieved enlightenment by analyzing waterfowl symbolism." She paused, then leaned in conspiratorially. "It’s his way of subtly testing our emotional stability, I swear. If we can dissect existential angst in avian metaphors, maybe we won't have to take English next year."

Maya let out a soft, breathy laugh. "You and your over-analysis. You act like you’re perpetually ready for a literature final, even on a Tuesday after school." She nudged Tiffany gently with her elbow. "Besides, it's not existential angst; it's just... well, it's poetry."

"Just because you can find the comfort in rhyme doesn't mean it isn't profound," Tiffany countered, though her tone was light, devoid of real judgment. "But fine, I’ll concede that some people just genuinely love the sound of words colliding. Like Chloe."

Maya’s easy smile faltered for the briefest second as she shifted her weight. "Chloe's cool, but she's not into poems. She's more into making sure my piano practice doesn't devolve into complete sonic chaos at midnight."

Tiffany nodded, understanding the subtle shift. It wasn't a big deal—just an observation about their circle—but she kept up the buoyant energy. "Yeah, true. We’d probably drive past Beth's house tomorrow and hear one of those dramatic Chopin pieces echoing down the street while Tony is trying to sleep off his graveyard shift."

Maya giggled this time, the memory easing the slight tension. "Or maybe he won't be asleep if I bring him decent coffee. He pretends he sleeps like a baby when he wakes up looking like a zombie who lost a fight with a lawnmower."

"He does look like that sometimes," Tiffany agreed readily. "Especially after those double shifts. But hey, we need fuel. My brain needs something other than lukewarm tap water by the time we get to dinner later." She gestured toward the cluster of familiar roadside businesses ahead. "Think the gas station has those ridiculously bright blue slushies? The kind that taste mostly like sugar and regret?"

"Probably," Maya confirmed, pushing off from the curb as they started heading closer to the commercial strip where the pumps were located. "I could use something icy. School today was... long."
