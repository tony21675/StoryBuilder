The late afternoon sun cast long shadows down the cracked pavement of the road between the school and the convenience of the nearby gas station. Tiffany adjusted her pace slightly, matching Maya’s easy stride as they walked. The air smelled like dry grass mingling with exhaust fumes.

"I don't know how I survived Mr. Harrison's history class," Tiffany said, kicking lightly at a loose pebble near the curb. She glanced at Maya, waiting for a shared grimace.

Maya let out a low, breathy laugh. "If he makes you write one more essay analyzing the socio-economic impact of medieval agricultural reform, I might just stage a dramatic fainting spell right there in the hall."

"Me too! It felt less like learning history and more like deciphering an ancient curse written by someone who clearly hated joy," Tiffany agreed, shaking her head. Her long, dark red curls bounced against her shoulders as she moved.

Maya shifted her weight, looking ahead toward the blinking sign visible further down the road—the one advertising cheap coffee and lottery tickets. "At least it's almost freedom. Coffee time is coming."

"Or snacks," Tiffany countered with a grin. "I saw that place has those ridiculously oversized bags of cheesy chips. They make everything taste like victory."

Maya smiled back, a genuine, relaxed curve to her lips. "You always go for the theatrics, Tiff. You want the maximal experience."

"Hey!" Tiffany protested playfully, nudging Maya’s arm lightly with her shoulder. "It's called commitment to flavor profile. Besides, victory tastes better when it involves artificial cheese dust."

They walked on, the rhythmic sound of their sneakers hitting the asphalt filling the comfortable silence between comments. Maya was quiet for a moment, watching the way the light caught the dusty gold in her hair, before kicking up her own small commentary.

"Do you think Chloe will be up tomorrow?" Maya asked quietly, shifting her focus slightly off the immediate surroundings.

Tiffany paused her stride just enough to look at her friend properly. She knew Chloe wasn't feeling well, but the mention of her felt heavy, even if it was only a minor thought. "Hopefully she sleeps it off. I don't want her having another one of those 'too energetic when sick' phases where she starts trying to convince us we need emergency soup rations."

Maya let out a soft chuckle, clearly amused by Tiffany’s imagined scenario. "I'd probably prefer the emergency soup rations anyway. At least that’s predictable."

As they rounded the last corner onto the access road leading into the station’s parking area, the brightly lit canopy of the gas station came fully into view, its fluorescent lights humming against the cooling late afternoon air. They were close now.

Tiffany slowed her walking pace slightly, coming to a stop beside Maya as they reached the paved entrance to the lot. Both girls stood still, looking toward the glass doors of the convenience store under the glowing sign.
