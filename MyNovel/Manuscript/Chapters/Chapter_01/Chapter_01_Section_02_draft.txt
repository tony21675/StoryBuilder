The gentle rhythm of their walk slowed subtly as the paved road began to widen and take on the characteristic gravel crunch of a commercial service area. The buildings lining the street—a mix of small storefronts, a dry cleaner, and other low-slung businesses—seemed to give way to the larger, more industrial footprint of the gas station complex.

"So, what are you doing this weekend?" Tiffany finally asked, her tone shifting from nostalgic reflection to light planning. She seemed to absorb the energy of the approaching destination, using the impending distraction of sodas and gossip to anchor the conversation.

Maya glanced up at the building signage overhead—the familiar, slightly faded colors of the fuel station branding—and nodded toward the general direction of the entrance. "Nothing major planned. Probably just binge-watching something terrible with Mom." She paused, then added softly, "You feeling okay? You seem... a little quiet since lunch period, even if we’re talking about slushies."

Tiffany shrugged, rolling her eyes in an affectionate gesture that Maya understood perfectly. "Oh, come on. I'm fine. Just tired. School is brutal, M. My brain feels like cotton wool dipped in glitter glue." She chuckled again, trying to redirect the focus away from any subtle shifts in mood. She felt a little too exposed in the late afternoon sun, suddenly aware of the casual nature of their conversation against the backdrop of the mundane roadside bustle.

Maya watched her friend for a beat longer than necessary, her observant nature picking up on the slight tension around Tiffany’s shoulders, despite the joking façade. Instead of pressing the issue, she let it drift, deciding instead to match Tiffany’s lighter tone before they reached the immediate vicinity of the gas station. "Well, maybe after these sugary drinks, you can tell me all about the history paper theories," Maya suggested, nudging him playfully when he started to veer off topic regarding a mutual acquaintance. "I need the good gossip mixed with the historical context."

They navigated the last stretch of sidewalk bordering the parking lot entrance. The air temperature seemed to change slightly as they moved closer to the pumps; the scent of gasoline, sharp and metallic, began to mingle with the sweet notes clinging to their clothing—the citrus-amber blend near Chloe’s absence, the soft vanilla mingling with mint, and the coconut-strawberry perfume trailing behind Tiffany.

The sidewalk narrowed just as they approached the main service area. The asphalt gave way to concrete curbing that bordered the main flow of traffic leading into the pumps. They passed the edge where the pedestrian walkway terminated and the commercial property truly began. Ahead, visible now in clear detail, was the structure of the gas station itself—the canopy supports, the illuminated signs promising various fuels, and the small cluster of storefront access points.

Tiffany slowed her pace almost imperceptibly, matching Maya’s rhythm as they stepped off the narrow sidewalk and onto the wider, designated pavement section right at the threshold of the gas station complex. They were no longer on the general connecting road; they had arrived at the mouth of the facility, standing just outside the immediate activity zone, facing the array of pumps and the attendant booth in the distance.
