Stepping across the threshold into the convenience store felt like transitioning from the warm, fading light of the late afternoon into a cooler, artificially lit bubble. The air inside was heavily scented—a competing mix of stale coffee grounds, sugary syrups, and disinfectant cleaner.

"Okay, hydration break," Tiffany announced, dropping her backpack near a cluster of magazine racks. She immediately gravitated toward the refrigerated drink section with Maya trailing close behind. "We need maximum sugar intake to counteract Mrs. Henderson's breath grading system."

Maya laughed softly, glancing around at the snack displays. "You always manage to frame something mundane as a vital survival mission, Tiff." She scanned the coolers, pointing out a variety of brightly colored sodas. "Just pick what you want. We can figure out the 'best' option later."

The attendant, a man in an apron, looked up from his register screen, offered a polite nod, and seemed ready for business.

Tiffany leaned over the selection, her finger hovering over a bottle of cherry-flavored soda, then paused. "Wait, do we get root beer? Or is that too much work?" she mused aloud to Maya, more to herself than to the attendant.

"Root beer sounds aggressively nostalgic right now," Maya murmured, though she found it amusing how easily Tiffany could be diverted by a beverage choice. "Let’s just stick to classics. Something blue, maybe? Like, pure, unadulterated blue sugar."

The attendant chuckled lightly at their back-and-forth. "Everything's stocked today, ladies. Just let me know what you want, and I'll ring it up."

It was a quick, easy transaction. They selected a couple of sodas—one electric blue, one a vibrant red—and a bag of chips because, frankly, they deserved snacks after a long school day. The attendant rang them up with practiced efficiency.

As he handed back the change, Tiffany caught Maya's eye. "We should probably take care of this whole thing fast before I get distracted by anything vaguely fizzy," she whispered, a conspiratorial grin playing on her lips.

"Agreed," Maya confirmed, taking the drinks and the change carefully. She managed the transaction quickly, handing over the money while keeping an eye on the attendant so as not to monopolize his attention. The attendant efficiently bagged their purchase.

With the payment settled and the cold drinks secured, they gathered near the exit point. The rush of activity seemed to pull them toward the glass door leading back outside. Tiffany took the bags, careful not to bump into any display items, while Maya carried the cooler soda bottles.

They stepped back out onto the main pavement, blinking slightly as the bright late afternoon sun hit them again. The contrast was immediate; the coolness of the store gave way to the intense warmth of the open air. They stood just beyond the entrance threshold, the purchased goods cradled between them. Without consulting each other, both Maya and Tiffany instinctively reached into their bags or pockets, pulling out sunglasses—Tiffany’s more fashionable, Maya’s more practical—and settling them over their noses against the glare.
