The fluorescent lights inside the gas station flickered slightly as Tiffany and Maya pushed through the automatic doors, the sudden blast of cool, conditioned air hitting them. The scent of stale coffee and sugary snacks filled the small convenience store.

"I need something fizzy," Maya said, drifting toward the cooler near the back wall. She leaned against the edge of the refrigerated cases, scanning the labels with a thoughtful frown.

Tiffany followed her, navigating around a display of brightly colored chips. "Me too. Something strong enough to cut through late-afternoon existential dread." She paused in front of a row of craft sodas, tapping one thoughtfully. "Or maybe just really sweet."

Maya finally made her selection, pulling out a two-liter bottle of grape soda. "This always works," she declared, holding it up for inspection.

Tiffany surveyed the options again, her eyes catching a brand of citrus soda she vaguely remembered from when they were younger. "Oh, the neon orange ones are dependable. Remind me of summer picnics that inevitably ended with ants everywhere." She grabbed a similar bottle. "I’m going with these."

They walked toward the counter, placing their selections down. A woman with tired eyes and a name tag reading 'Brenda' looked up from wiping the glass behind the register.

"Find everything okay?" Brenda asked, not sounding particularly enthusiastic about the inquiry.

"Yep," Tiffany confirmed, leaning slightly on the counter. "Just the usual sugary fuel."

Maya chimed in, resting her elbows on the counter. "Yeah, thanks. Just need something cold after that history lecture."

Brenda scanned the drinks with practiced speed. "That'll be five-fifty."

"Here you go," Maya said, handing over a crumpled ten-dollar bill.

Brenda took the bill, punched it into the register, and started dispensing change, sliding the bottles into a plastic bag. "Have a good one."

"You too, Brenda," Tiffany replied easily, already mentally cataloging how much she liked the smell of the outside air compared to the interior.

With their sodas secured—Tiffany had opted for a different, equally vibrant orange soda—they shuffled out the automatic doors back onto the asphalt. The sunlight felt intensely bright after the dim interior.

Maya shifted the heavy two-liter bottle slightly, then reached into her pocket and pulled out a pair of sunglasses, slipping them onto her face. Tiffany mirrored the action, reaching into her own worn backpack to retrieve her own pair and settling them over her eyes as well. The glare bouncing off the parked cars was almost blinding.
