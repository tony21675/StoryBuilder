Tony remained upright against the headboard, his arms still securely wrapped around Maya’s slim frame, supporting her weight as she curled against his chest. The scent of cedar and worn leather, mixed with the faintest undercurrent of motor oil from his skin, settled around her like a comforting, familiar blanket, though the warmth wasn't enough to banish the rising tide of shock that threatened to pull them both under.

Maya felt the solid reality of him beneath her—the steady rhythm of his breathing, the reassuring strength of his hold—and clung to it, anchoring herself in the moment after having finally spilled out the devastating news: Tiffany was gone.

The silence that followed her whisper was heavy, broken only by the muted sounds filtering in from outside the house. Tony didn't flinch or recoil; instead, he seemed to absorb the word—*gone*—into his core, processing the seismic shift it represented for his daughter, for their family unit. His protective instincts, usually so measured, flickered visibly across his hazel eyes before settling back into a deep, steady concern for *her*.

It was in this quiet vacuum of shared devastation that Tony’s attention snagged. As he adjusted slightly, shifting his grip just enough to peer down at her, his gaze drifted past her face, catching on something near her left cheek. There, faintly visible against the pale skin, was a temporary, angry-looking red mark.

His brow furrowed almost imperceptibly, the natural alertness that defined him snapping into focus. He gently shifted his head until his gaze rested entirely on the blemish, then up into Maya’s brown eyes. The professional calm that usually underpinned his demeanor began to crack around the edges, replaced by a sudden, acute focus on her well-being.

"Hey," he murmured, his voice rougher than usual, stripped bare of its usual dry wit. He didn't release her, but the slight movement allowed him to cup the side of her jaw with one hand, his thumb brushing lightly against the raised redness. "Maya? What happened here?"
