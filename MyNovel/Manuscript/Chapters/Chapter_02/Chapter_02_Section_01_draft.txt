Maya remained curled against Tony’s chest, drawing comfort from the solid warmth of his body despite the roaring chaos still echoing in her mind. The vanilla scent of her fear mixed faintly with the comforting cedar and soap notes emanating from Tony. He held her tightly, one arm across her back, the other supporting her weight as she clung to him like this.

Tony’s own breathing was steadying, deep, measured breaths that seemed to act as anchors in the sudden storm of reality. His strong hands felt capable, reassuring, but Maya could sense beneath the practiced calm there a tremor he was fighting to suppress. He stroked her hair rhythmically, his gaze fixed on the expanse of the bedroom wall rather than meeting her eyes, as if looking anywhere else would confirm the terrible thing she had just said.

"She's gone," Maya whispered again, the words feeling too small, too insignificant for the vast, gaping hole where Tiffany used to be. The admission was ripped out of her, raw and ragged.

The silence that followed wasn't empty; it was thick, weighted down by disbelief and panic. Tony didn't respond immediately. Instead, his movements faltered slightly, the steady rhythm of his stroking halting. Slowly, carefully, Tony shifted his grip, allowing him a better view of her face. As he adjusted his angle, his attention snagged.

His brow furrowed, and his hand stilled near her cheek. He brought his thumb up, brushing lightly against the skin just beside her jawline, where a faint, angry-looking red mark was visible, contrasting sharply with her pale complexion.

He finally looked down at Maya, his hazel eyes narrowing with concern—the protective instinct overriding the shock of losing Tiffany entirely. "Whoa. Hey. What happened here?" His voice was low, remarkably even despite the catastrophe unfolding around them. "Your cheek. What happened to your face, May?"
