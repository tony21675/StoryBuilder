Tony kept one arm securely looped around Maya’s waist, his other hand resting lightly on her back, careful not to jostle her too much. He continued to hold her close, the familiar scent of clean soap, cedar, and worn leather mixing with the sweet cloud of vanilla that clung to her. His hazel eyes held an unwavering depth of concern as he studied the faint redness tracing across her cheekbone.

“Hey,” he murmured, his voice low and steady, a practiced tone meant to soothe panic without sounding patronizing. “Talk to me, okay? About that mark.”

Maya didn't answer immediately. She remained curled against him, drawing comfort from the solid weight of his body. Her breathing was shallow, ragged little sighs punctuated by small, choked catches in her throat. The adrenaline crash was hitting her hard, turning the edges of reality fuzzy and unreliable. She kept her head tucked into the curve of his neck, inhaling the comforting, masculine scent that grounded her where she needed it most.

Finally, a shaky breath escaped her. She lifted her gaze slightly, just enough so that her brown eyes could meet his worried look, but she couldn't force herself to fully sit up or straighten. “It… it happened out there,” she whispered, the words costing her visible effort. She swallowed hard, tasting copper and panic. “When... when I got separated. Near the road.”

Tony waited patiently, letting the silence stretch just long enough for her to gather the strength, not pressuring her with more questions. He merely tilted his head fractionally, encouraging her to continue at her own pace.

A shudder ran through Maya, causing her to press closer into him as if seeking physical reassurance against the shock. She squeezed her eyes shut again, and he felt the tension ripple through her slight frame.

“The man,” she started, her voice barely audible, laced with an almost childlike tremor. She bit her lip hard enough to draw a bead of blood, which stung more than any external injury. She opened her eyes again, focusing fiercely on a point on Tony’s chest, anywhere but his face, as if looking away would help her recall the details in order.

“Tiffany was… she was fighting,” Maya managed, the memory pulling itself from her like frayed thread. Her description was patchy, emotional rather than sequential, yet horribly vivid in its impact. “He grabbed her right when we were walking. And she fought back. Remember? The moves you taught her.” A flicker of a grim smile touched her lips before it vanished, replaced by sudden distress.

Tony listened intently, every muscle in his body coiled with readiness, though he remained physically still to support her. He kept one hand steadying her hip, providing an unshakeable anchor.

“I tried to help,” Maya continued, ignoring the sharp ache in her cheek for a moment because recounting the sequence was paramount. “But then... he turned. Towards me. I just froze for a second. Like I couldn't—I couldn’t move or think.” She hated that admission; it felt weak, betraying the strong girl who had witnessed everything.

“And then Tiffany scratched him,” she mumbled, her voice gaining a tiny fraction of strength as she recalled the action that hurt him. “Three times. On his left cheek. You could see the blood coming out.” She didn't elaborate on how it looked, only that it *had* bled.

The effort of recalling the brutality seemed to exhaust her entirely. Her grip on Tony’s shirt tightened suddenly, almost painfully.

“Then… he hit me,” she whispered, the word 'hit' sounding alien and wrong in the quiet room. Tears welled instantly, spilling over onto the fabric of his shirt, dampening the scent of cedar and soap around his collarbone. “It stung. Really badly. And then...” She trailed off, shaking her head slowly, trying to push past the immediate physical pain to the next event. “He threw her. Through the open side door of the van.”

Tony absorbed every word, his protective instincts rising like heat from a hearth. His jaw tightened imperceptibly beneath his beard, but he kept his expression soft, radiating an unshakeable calm meant to encourage her recollection rather than reacting to the trauma itself. He leaned down just slightly, letting his warm breath brush against her temple. “Take your time, Maya. Whatever you remember, that’s enough right now.”

Maya blinked rapidly, the tears momentarily distracting her focus. She struggled through the final, most impactful images. “And he drove away. With her inside.” The statement was flat, devoid of energy, as if the sheer fact of it—the finality of it—had sucked all the air out of her lungs. She finally slumped against him, utterly depleted, leaving the image of the receding vehicle hanging heavy in the late afternoon quiet of the bedroom.
