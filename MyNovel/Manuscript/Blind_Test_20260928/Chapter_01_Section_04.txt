Tony's phone buzzed twice against the nightstand before dying out, leaving only the soft ticking of the antique clock across the room as his sole company. The air smelled of old wood, dust, and the faintest hint of motor oil from the garage – scents that had become familiar over years of quiet evenings spent watching television or reading by lamp light. He'd been awake longer than intended, tossing between sleep and wakefulness when his daughter finally stopped texting him late into the evening. Now he was buried under thick blankets, wrapped in the kind of deep rest that came only after exhaustion and stress had run their course.

The door burst open so violently it nearly knocked over a stack of books beside the bed.

"Tony! Tony!" Maya's voice cracked like thunder in the small bedroom, raw with panic and barely containing something deeper. "Tiffany – they took Tiffany! I saw them, I'm sorry, please, listen!"

He sat bolt upright instantly, heart hammering against his ribs as she stumbled through the doorway, eyes wild, hair disheveled, face pale with fear. She didn't look at him properly – not really – just kept scanning past him toward where he must have been standing seconds ago, still processing the nightmare that had overtaken her best friend.

"I didn't see where they were taking her," Maya continued, voice rising despite herself, "but I know it's happening right now. Chloe called me saying she saw someone leave with her in some kind of van, and then she got scared and ran back here. But – but Tiffany isn't answering her phone anymore. And my mom says there's no call on any of the numbers I've tried."

Her hands trembled as they gripped the edge of the doorway, knuckles white. The scent of clean laundry and soft vanilla hung around her, mixed with fresh mint from somewhere inside the house next door where she'd clearly come running straight from.

Tony rubbed his temples briefly before meeting her gaze fully, seeing beyond the immediate crisis to the girl who had grown up watching him change diapers, who had confided in him about crushes and school problems during sleepovers when parents were out. This wasn't just a neighbor calling for help; this was Maya – his Maya – unraveling piece by piece.

"Easy, kid," he said gently, reaching out instinctively even though he knew better than to touch her yet. She flinched slightly at the motion but stayed in place, breathing too fast. "Tell me what you're seeing. What did you see?"
