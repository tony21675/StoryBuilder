The bell's final shrill echoed through the classroom doors as Tiffany slipped her backpack onto one shoulder and pushed against the worn wood to exit. Outside, late afternoon light caught the dust motes dancing above the cracked asphalt of the schoolyard.

"Ready?" Maya called from behind, adjusting the strap of her own bag.

"You know I am," Tiffany said with a grin as she turned back. The familiar weight of Tony's dog tags felt reassuring against her collarbone beneath her shirt. They'd walked this route countless times since they were babies—first with their parents, then just the two of them when Tiffany was old enough to walk.

They fell into step along the front road that cut between Tony's house and Maya's. The air smelled faintly of cut grass and the distant hum of traffic on Highway 101. A few scattered students drifted past, heading toward different destinations.

"How's your mom doing?" Tiffany asked as they rounded the corner near the banyan tree where they used to play hide-and-seek.

Maya glanced at her, studying her expression for any signs of strain. "Same as always," she replied after a pause. "She's in the kitchen making dinner. You'll be home soon, so she's probably planning something special."

Tiffany nodded, her fingers tracing the chain around her neck. "I should tell her I'm home when I get there."

"Of course you will," Maya said, bumping her shoulder lightly. "You always do."

A small park sat ahead, its playground equipment growing rusty in the summer heat. Children's laughter drifted from within, mingling with the sound of crickets beginning their evening chorus.

"Do you think it'll rain tonight?" Tiffany wondered aloud, looking up at the clouds gathering on the horizon.

"I don't know," Maya admitted. "The sky looks... overcast. But not like it's going to pour. Just kind of heavy."

They continued walking, the pavement warming under their sneakers. The air carried the smell of gasoline from somewhere nearby, mixed with the scent of blooming jasmine from Tony's front yard that was visible through the fence.

"I heard Mr. Henderson is giving a presentation tomorrow," Maya mentioned, her voice dropping slightly. "About renewable energy sources or something."

"That sounds boring," Tiffany teased, though she knew Maya appreciated learning things like this.

"It's not boring," Maya defended herself. "It's important. We need to understand these things now so we can make better choices later."

Tiffany laughed softly. "Maybe you'll be an engineer someday."

"Who knows?" Maya shrugged, though her eyes sparkled with possibility. "If I had my way, I'd study psychology. Or maybe social work."

"You want to help people," Tiffany said simply.

Maya nodded seriously. "People need understanding more than anything else. Sometimes they just feel alone and don't realize how much support there is around them."

They reached the intersection where the road split toward different neighborhoods. Left led home for both of them eventually; right would take them past several blocks to other homes.

"We'll go left," Tiffany decided, already knowing what direction their houses were in. She pointed down the familiar street lined with palm trees.

As they approached the gas station sign ahead—a bright blue rectangle mounted on poles—they paused at the crosswalk. Cars passed slowly, their headlights cutting through the gathering dusk.

"Do you remember that time we got lost after school?" Tiffany asked suddenly, remembering a specific incident that always made them laugh when they thought about it.

Maya smiled. "The one where we ended up at the wrong house and had to borrow someone's phone? Yes, I remember perfectly."

"That was so embarrassing!" Tiffany covered her face dramatically, though she couldn't suppress her laughter.

"It wasn't that bad," Maya said, but her smile betrayed her amusement. "And you did call your dad when we needed help finding our way back."

"I still can't believe you didn't recognize Mr. Thompson's house," Tiffany chided gently. "His car has those distinctive green stripes on the side."

"And his dog always sits by the front door," Maya added, picturing the scene as if she could see it. "Remember how he'd wave when we walked by? 'Hello, kids!'"

They crossed the street now, the pavement cool underfoot despite the approaching evening. The gas station's convenience store lights were already flickering on inside, casting a warm glow against the glass windows.

"Do you want something from the store before we go home?" Maya suggested. "I'm thirsty."

Tiffany considered for a moment. She reached into her pocket and pulled out a crumpled wrapper—she'd been saving money all week for something specific. But seeing Maya's tired eyes made her reconsider.

"What do you want?" she asked instead.

"Water," Maya replied immediately. "Just water would be fine."

"Okay," Tiffany agreed, pulling out some coins from her jeans. "We can get one bottle each."

Inside the store, fluorescent lights hummed overhead as they browsed the aisles. Maya picked up a plastic water bottle while Tiffany grabbed another. They placed them in the cart together, chatting about nothing much—a movie they might watch later, whether Tony had finished his homework yet, if anyone had noticed any changes at school.

At the checkout counter, Maya handed over payment with precise calculation. The cashier smiled politely without comment. As they left carrying their purchases, Tiffany felt the familiar sensation of being safe walking home with her best friend.

The walk continued toward their destination now, the gas station receding behind them like a brief stop on an otherwise ordinary evening.
