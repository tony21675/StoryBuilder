The last of the afternoon light bled orange against the sky when Tiffany and Maya stepped off the front road and onto the gravel shoulder beside the gas station. The air smelled faintly of gasoline and warm pavement, but mostly, it just felt like summer air—thick, quiet, full of cicadas.

They walked in silence for a few steps, each lost in their own thoughts, before Maya finally broke it. "You okay?" she asked, her voice low.

Tiffany didn't answer right away. She just kept walking, her gaze fixed on the tall weeds along the roadside. Her fingers brushed absently at the dog tags resting beneath her shirt—a habit she couldn’t break, even now that there was no one to show them to.

"I’m fine," she said eventually. "Just tired."

Maya nodded like she understood perfectly—which she did. They’d known each other since they were babies; some things didn’t need saying out loud.

For a while, they walked side by side, shoulders brushing occasionally, until they reached the corner where Maya’s house sat next door to Tony’s. There was still time—plenty of time—for whatever came next.
