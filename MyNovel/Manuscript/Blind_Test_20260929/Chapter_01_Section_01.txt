The final bell had rung less than ten minutes ago, but the hallways were already emptying. The usual crush of students was gone, replaced by a steady stream of lockers slamming shut and backpacks hitting shoulders as groups scattered like leaves in a sudden wind.

Tiffany caught up to Maya near the main exit, her own bag slung over one shoulder. "You got everything?" Tiffany asked, pulling out her phone to check her messages.

"I'm good," Maya said, checking her own schedule on her phone. "Just need my lunch from the locker room." She reached for her bag. "Wait—my keys are in my jacket pocket. Did you see me put them there after we left homeroom?"

"Right, right," Tiffany said. They both paused to rummage through pockets. After a minute of that, Tiffany's face lit up. "There! Found 'em!"

Maya smiled, taking the keys and slipping them into her own pocket. They fell into step outside, walking down the school's front road together. The late afternoon sun hit their backs with that warm, low angle that made everything look soft and golden.

"You want me to get your lunch or mine?" Maya asked casually as they walked.

"Nah, I'll grab something at the gas station," Tiffany said. "I'm starved though. I didn't even think about eating until now."

"I know that feeling," Maya laughed softly. "It's always either too late or way too early."

They passed by parked cars lining the street, some with kids' seats still buckled in, others with groceries piled high in the backseats. A dog barked somewhere near an open garage door.

"It's funny how quiet it gets after everyone leaves," Maya mused. "Like the whole place exhales."

Tiffany nodded. "Yeah, it feels... empty, but peaceful. No more shouting, no more running around." She glanced at her friend. "You okay with going straight home?"

"Totally," Maya said easily. "I was just thinking about dinner—my mom's making lasagna tonight. I can't wait."

Tiffany smiled. "Your mom sounds amazing."

"She is!" Maya said warmly. "She makes this incredible tomato sauce from scratch. And she taught me how to roll my own pasta."

"That sounds like a lot of fun."

"It really is," Maya continued. "We spend hours in the kitchen on Sunday mornings sometimes. It's kind of our thing."

As they walked toward the gas station, the air smelled faintly of cut grass and warm asphalt. The road ahead showed them clearly: the familiar sign for "QuickStop & Go" just up ahead, its bright red letters glowing against the fading light.

"We should check out that new coffee shop downtown sometime," Tiffany suggested. "Saw it last week."

"Yeah, maybe," Maya said thoughtfully. "But we'd have to pick another night—I'm busy studying most evenings now."

"I know," Tiffany agreed. They kept their pace steady, comfortable silence settling between them as they approached the station's entrance.
