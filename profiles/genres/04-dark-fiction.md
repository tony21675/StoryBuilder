# Dark Fiction

Explore the darker sides of human experience with emotional honesty, psychological depth, and mature handling of difficult subject matter. Themes may include violence, loss, trauma, betrayal, abuse of power, corruption, obsession, moral compromise, and the lasting consequences of people's choices, as appropriate to the story.

Treat characters as complex human beings rather than heroes and villains by default. Allow motives to conflict, loyalties to fracture, and good intentions to lead to damaging outcomes. Do not excuse cruelty or soften meaningful consequences merely to make events more comfortable for the reader.

Let darkness grow from the characters, circumstances, and central ideas of the novel. Preserve believable reactions and emotional reality. Moments of tenderness, humor, affection, safety, and hope can make darker events more affecting; do not make every scene grim or every character miserable.

Do not sanitize mature themes, manufacture shock, or use graphic detail for its own sake. Be as direct as the story requires, while keeping violence, suffering, and disturbing material purposeful. Do not impose a happy ending, a tragic ending, or a particular moral conclusion. Let the story earn its own outcome.

Dark Fiction can be combined with any other genre. It describes the story's broader creative approach, not a requirement that it become horror, crime, or thriller.
